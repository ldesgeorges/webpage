Research

My research is carried out at the LIP laboratory (ENS de Lyon, CNRS, Université Claude Bernard Lyon 1), in the HoWNeT team.

Research interests

Security of SDN control

Introducing security into the SDN Controller Placement Problem, in particular for O-RAN architectures, and taking into account the ability to detect anomalies when placing controllers.

Observation of SDN control

Observing the activity of SDN controllers in order to detect anomalies in their decisions, from a single controller to distributed controllers.

Privacy of geolocation systems

Understanding the privacy risks of geolocation ecosystems and designing privacy-preserving geolocation and tracking architectures.

Optimization in wireless networks

Energy saving in Wi-Fi networks through Target Wake Time (TWT), and control of a drone fleet for network coverage using reinforcement learning.

PhD thesis

Détection d'anomalies de sûreté et sécurité d'un contrôle centralisé de réseau (Detection of safety and security anomalies in a centralized network control), written in French and defended on 17 November 2022.

The thesis was carried out at the CRAN laboratory (Université de Lorraine, Nancy), in the ISET team, under the supervision of Thierry Divoux and Jean-Philippe Georges. It was funded by the Lorraine Université d'Excellence initiative (LUE I-SITE), within the IMPACT project "DigiTrust".

Software-Defined Networking (SDN) relies on centralized control, which makes the safety and security of the controller essential. Multi-controller solutions reinforce the control layer, but they require an interface between controllers, which an attacker can use to propagate erroneous information. The thesis proposes a control architecture made of a nominal controller and an observer in charge of detecting anomalies in the controller's decisions, by studying only the activity of the controller. It introduces specifications, temporal and structural properties, and models to analyze the likelihood of the controller's solutions. A likelihood score is associated with each observation through a multi-criteria approach, based on the performance of the plans and the likelihood of the routes. The results show that the proposed methods are applicable, with some limitations, and lay the foundations of a detection method and, more generally, of a network observability concept.
